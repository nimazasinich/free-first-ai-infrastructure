# Flash account latency investigation

## Decision

- **PASS — Account association.** Flash latency differed sharply by finishing account in the retained native journal, including after the reinstall and among requests with comparable logged parameters. Both Pro-scoped accounts that finished requests showed the slow pattern. The sample cannot establish an effect for all Pro accounts or establish that subscription tier caused the delay.
- **PASS — Timing localization.** Native AIStudio2API spent 303.616 seconds in its Playground response-header wait on the fresh long request. It recorded 0.551 ms of queue time and 53.901 ms of WAA proof time. Root cause within the upstream request remains **UNKNOWN**.
- **BLOCKED — Routing experiment.** The evidence supports a separately authorized, isolated native account-scope trial. A production-wide Flash binding still requires authorization and capacity evidence. Current production bindings and client configuration were preserved.

## Routing and capacity

**PASS — Effective route verified.** The official GUI API reported seven aliases, no real Flash `MODEL_ACCOUNTS` entry, and empty `API_KEY_ACCOUNTS` scope for the active native configuration. Round-robin, three non-quota account switches, Playground-only channels, no model channel overrides, hedge off, and SpiderMonkey were effective. Alias resolution happens before native model selection. The tagged selection code intersects the native key restriction when configured and applies the real-model restriction to unpinned selection. Explicit account or resource affinity changes the latter. The ordinary unbound Flash route was eligible to draw from both groups, subject to model support, readiness and cooldown. The journal confirms it drew from both. [Official Settings reference](https://github.com/Mag1cFall/AIStudio2API/blob/v0.3.12/README_en.md) · [Account selection implementation](https://github.com/Mag1cFall/AIStudio2API/blob/v0.3.12/internal/aistudio/channel.go)

At the fresh control check, six Free-scoped and two Pro-scoped seats were Ready. One seat in each group was Restricted, and zero cooldowns were active. A seven-account Free-only binding would cut the configured candidate pool from ten to seven and the currently Ready pool from eight to six. At two concurrent requests per account, the nominal Ready slots would move from 16 to 12 (**25% lower**). Those slots are a capacity ceiling. Sustained throughput and per-account quota budgets are **UNKNOWN**. Less account diversity increases the chance of cooling all candidates at once. The native scheduler can try other eligible Free seats under the same-model policy. It cannot spill to the removed Pro seats when the scope applies. Scoped cooldown/exhaustion may surface as queueing, rate-limit or no-eligible-account errors.

**BLOCKED — Proposed reversible experiment.** Obtain explicit owner authorization first. In the official UI, verify the existing key/model scope and eligible Ready seats. Prefer a distinct native test key with a Free-only account restriction and a local isolated test route, if the owner approves that key and isolation. A new public key against the existing shared native bridge credential cannot isolate account selection by itself. Use the existing scope as the control and the test key as the treatment. Run at most six sequential native generations total across the two lanes, one matched short stream and one two-request tool round trip per lane, with fixed prompts, reasoning, limits and timeouts. Disable client retries, preserve native failover bounds, count native attempts and retain timeouts as censored observations. Compare first event, terminal correctness, account distribution, 403/429/cooldown behavior and Ready slot counts. Stop for scope leakage, duplicate gateway forwards, or loss of correctness or capacity. Revoke the test key and restore its scope in the UI at the end. A favorable small trial is a safety and correctness check. Evaluate routine traffic and capacity separately before seeking a decision on production scope. Leave existing account, routing and billing controls in place during this investigation.
